# The national layer

**Verified 2026-09-14.** The EU/EEA baseline — ESPD, exclusion grounds, selection
criteria, award criteria — is common. What follows is what each country puts on
top of it, and it is where a cross-border bidder gets caught.

Priority order: Sweden, Norway, Denmark, then Estonia, then the rest.

---

## Sweden

- **Portal:** no single mandatory state portal. Buyers use commercial systems
  (Mercell, TendSign/Visma, e-Avrop, Kommers). Notices above threshold go to TED;
  national notices to a registered annonsdatabas.
- **Law:** LOU (lagen om offentlig upphandling, 2016:1145); LUF for utilities.
  Chapter 19 carries the simplified national rules, in force since February 2022.
- **Exclusion evidence:** Skatteverket's own form for tax and social-security
  status is the routine instrument; criminal-record evidence is handled through
  declarations rather than a single consolidated certificate.
- **Characteristic demand:** *sanningsförsäkran* — a truth declaration — plus
  heavy use of collective-agreement-equivalent labour terms (*arbetsrättsliga
  villkor*) in contract conditions.
- **Note for this offer:** Swedish suppliers in `_research/nordic-manufacturers/voice.md` complain
  more about **irrelevant requirements and price dominance** than about document
  format. 73 per cent of small companies say irrelevant requirements are often
  set (Företagarna, 1,114 respondents, 2023).

## Norway (EEA)

- **Portal:** **Doffin** is mandatory for national notices; TED above threshold.
- **Law:** anskaffelsesloven and **anskaffelsesforskriften (FOA)**, FOR-2016-08-12-974.
- **The distinguishing instrument:** **FOA § 7-9**, in force **1 January 2024** —
  climate and environment weighted **minimum 30 per cent** of award criteria,
  follow-or-explain, with a narrow exception where the procurement's environmental
  burden is insignificant, which must be justified in the procurement documents.
  This is the single hardest environmental-documentation driver in the region and
  it is already two years and eight months old.
- **Labour conditions:** forskrift om lønns- og arbeidsvilkår i offentlige
  kontrakter is the strictest regional regime. Expect documentation obligations
  that extend down the subcontractor chain.
- **Tax certificate:** *skatteattest* is the standard exclusion evidence.
  **Verify the current threshold and whether HMS-egenerklæring survives** — much
  published guidance still describes the repealed 2001/2006 regulations. Flagged
  in `refresh.md`.
- **Prequalification:** StartBANK and Achilles JQS/Sellihca are widely used in
  energy, utilities and construction.

## Denmark

- **Portal:** **udbud.dk** for national publication; TED above threshold. Buyers
  run commercial tender systems (Mercell, EU-Supply, Ethics).
- **Law:** udbudsloven.
- **The distinguishing instrument:** the **serviceattest** from Erhvervsstyrelsen
  — a single consolidated certificate pulling ATP, the tax authority, the probate
  court and the criminal register into one document. It is the cleanest
  exclusion-evidence instrument in the region and there is no direct equivalent
  in the other four countries. Order it early; it takes time to issue and foreign
  management members require ID and a consent declaration.
- **Sequence:** ESPD is preliminary evidence. Final documentation is normally
  demanded **only from the winning bidder, immediately before award**. A supplier
  who prepares the full evidence pack for every bid is doing work the law does
  not require — a genuine, checkable saving worth knowing.
- **Complaint route:** Klagenævnet for Udbud. Its rulings are public, full-text
  and reproduce the complainant's own arguments — the richest supplier-voice
  archive located anywhere in this research.

## Estonia

- **Portal:** **Riigihangete register (RHR)**, riigihanked.riik.ee, run by the
  Ministry of Finance. **Mandatory** — all procedures above the statutory
  threshold run through it, and it is free to access. More centralised than
  anything in Scandinavia.
- **Law:** riigihangete seadus (RHS).
- **ESPD equivalent:** **hankepass**, issued and completed inside RHR. Buyers
  publish qualification and exclusion conditions as *hankepass täiendavate
  selgitustega* — hankepass with additional clarifications.
- **Register structure:** the tender base documents carry four blocks —
  qualification conditions, conformity conditions, evaluation criteria, cost.
  That structure is a useful skeleton for an Estonian submission.
- **Complaint route:** VAKO (vaidlustuskomisjon).
- **Note:** Estonia is digitally the most integrated of the five. Much evidence
  is pulled from state registers rather than supplied by the bidder, which
  shrinks Tier 0 and shifts weight onto Tier 2.

## Finland

- **Portal:** **HILMA** (hankintailmoitukset.fi) for national publication; TED
  above threshold.
- **Law:** hankintalaki (1397/2016).
- **The distinguishing instrument:** **tilaajavastuulaki** — the Act on the
  Contractor's Obligations and Liability. Where it applies, the buyer must obtain
  and the supplier must provide evidence **no more than three months old**:
  register entries, tax-debt status or a *verovelkatodistus*, and proof of pension
  insurance taken out and paid. Non-compliance exposes the *buyer* to a negligence
  fee of EUR 2,000 to 20,000, which is why Finnish buyers chase it hard.
  **Luotettava Kumppani** (Vastuu Group) is the commercial service that compiles
  it automatically — widely used and effectively an expected credential.
- **Complaint route:** markkinaoikeus, the Market Court. Judgments are public and
  reproduce the complainant's arguments.

## Iceland, Latvia, Lithuania — lower priority

- **Iceland (EEA):** Útboðsvefur / Ríkiskaup. Small market, EEA-aligned.
- **Latvia:** `_research/nordic-manufacturers/voice.md` records that **73 per cent of awards
  2021–2023 were decided on lowest price alone** (FICIL). A document-and-quality
  offer has a much smaller addressable slice here. The profile explicitly
  excludes Latvia and it should stay excluded until that figure moves.
- **Lithuania:** CVP IS portal. FICIL ranks Lithuania's fairness problems as
  materially less pronounced than Latvia's. Not researched in depth.

## The cross-border tool

**e-Certis** maps which national certificate satisfies which ESPD criterion in
each member state. It is the correct first stop for "what does Denmark accept
instead of our Finnish certificate" and it covers EU members; the EEA states are
less completely represented.
