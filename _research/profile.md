### 2. The trigger

### A1. Section 2 — one instrument was missing, and it does not do what it first appeared to

`[VERIFIED]` The **Construction Products Regulation (EU) 2024/3110** is absent
from section 2's list of five dated instruments. It applies generally from
**8 January 2026**.

`[VERIFIED]` **It creates no obligation for any manufacturer yet.** Article 95(9)
applies the obligations on economic operators to a product family only from **one
year after adoption of an implementing act making a harmonised standard
mandatory**. Article 92 brings penalties, including for environmental performance
declarations, from **8 January 2027** — enforcement machinery, not an obligation
start. As of 2026-09-15 three standardisation requests had been adopted (precast
concrete; structural metallic products and ancillaries; cement and hydraulic
binders — two of them still under the 2011 Regulation) and four more were in
preparation. A standardisation request is neither a harmonised standard nor an
implementing act. **No product family has started its one-year clock.**

**Consequence: section 2's reading survives intact.** Adding CPR does not add
urgency. It adds a sixth instrument that is announced and not yet binding, which
is the same shape as most of the other five.

### A2. Section 2 — the September 2026 instrument is a Regulation, and a proposal

`[VERIFIED]` The Public Procurement Act presented 9 September 2026 is a proposed
**single Regulation intended to replace Directives 2014/24/EU, 2014/25/EU and
2014/23/EU**, not an amendment to them. A Regulation applies directly with no
national transposition, which removes the member-state discretion that currently
shapes how each Nordic country runs procurement. Quality at minimum 30 per cent,
50 per cent for labour-intensive contracts, on a comply-or-explain basis, with
best price-quality ratio as the standard award method.

`[VERIFIED]` **It has passed neither Parliament nor Council.** Section 2 describes
it as presented, which is correct; nothing downstream should describe it as law.

`[INFERRED]` It reaches **Norway and Iceland only through EEA incorporation, on a
separate and later timetable.** Section 11 and the handoff treat the geography as
one regulatory unit. It is not one. Unverified as to timing.
