## The plan

**Shape — mechanism first.** Chosen from awareness, not preference. The reader
knows the problem cold and has been saying so for years in five languages. What
they do not have is the cause. Problem-first would spend the page describing a
burden they can describe better than I can. Mechanism first lets recognition do
the work, then locates their own losses inside the new frame.

**The opening is a fact, not a claim.** A market at stage three on the claim will
reject a seventh assertion. It cannot reject a published ruling with a case
number. The page therefore opens on the record and lets the reader draw the
conclusion.

**Big idea, stated without naming the product.** *Public buyers score the
documents you hand in, not the company you run, which is why a bid that is
cheapest and fastest can still lose.*

**The gradient.**

- **Low position, sourced.** You bid, you lost, nobody told you which part cost
  you the points, and you concluded it was price. Traceable to the client answer
  of 2026-09-16 and to *"Jag påpekade brister men det är ändå lägsta pris som
  avgör"*.
- **High position, `[ENTAILED]` and written flat.** Nothing in three passes and
  five languages describes the state after. The page states only what the offer
  strictly produces. A set of documents exists, your own people hold it, and the
  next submission is edited rather than assembled. **No relief, no confidence, no
  growth.** An entailed ceiling that gets dressed up becomes an invented one.

**Sequence, and what each section owes the next.**

1. The record. Owes the next section the question *so what was I being scored on*
2. The mechanism. Owes the reader their own case
3. Their position inside it. Provokes *it is all price anyway*, so that objection
   is answered next
4. The price objection, answered with Norway and conceded with Latvia. Owes the
   reader *then what is the thing you sell*
5. What a document standard is, and what it is not. Provokes *what will this cost
   me in hours*
6. What it needs from you, named precisely. Owes the reader the price
7. Price and the ladder. Provokes *we only bid a few, and only locally*
8. That objection, answered. Owes the action
9. The action
10. The close, repeating the hero word for word

**Objection placement.** Mechanism doubt (*it is all price*) at section 4, where
the copy causes it. Belief in self (*we bid three times a year, locally*) at
section 8, beside the arithmetic that provokes it. Cost and effort (*free
consulting we cannot afford*) at section 6, before the price rather than after.

**Proof — level 1 and level 2, and nothing below.** Demonstration, via the scored
page, which is the action itself. Verifiable specifics, via two published rulings
with case numbers and dates and one dated national weighting rule. **There are no
testimonials and no client names on this page, because the research contains
none.** Assertion is not used anywhere.

**Register.** Authority. The opacity is the buyer's, not the reader's, and the
page never implies the reader has been foolish.

**The one action.** Get your page scored.

### Two notes, neither a contradiction

- *Scored* is the vocabulary of the thing the reader keeps losing at, and the
  research shows they resent being asked to prove themselves repeatedly. The draft
  answers this by scoring **a page they already have**, against **the criteria it
  was already judged on**, so nothing new is produced to be judged.
- The free entry earns its keep only if the page genuinely exists. The draft asks
  for a page from a tender already submitted, which removes the string that rung
  06 warns about. If in practice you would accept a page they have to write first,
  the entry point stops being free and the copy needs changing.

## What the file actually said

P. Olesen og Sønner bid for a demolition contract and lost it. They were the
cheapest bidder. They were also the fastest.

They lost on five days.

Not five days on the work. Five days on when they said the quality-assurance
paperwork would be handed over. The Danish complaints board published the case in
October 2025, number 25/03606, and their own sentence is in it.

*It makes no sense that so few days have had such great significance.*

Read that again, because the arithmetic is the point. The company was better on
the two things a buyer says it wants. It lost on a date inside a document.

## The evaluation scores the document

A Norwegian contractor made the same discovery from the other end. Mesta argued
to KOFA that the authority had not assessed whether it could do the work at all.
It had only assessed how each supplier scored on the documentation requirements.
Mesta also said it had been marked down for things that were never asked for.

That is two countries, two industries, and one finding. What gets scored is the
paperwork, and the paperwork is a separate object from the company.

This is not a complaint about fairness. It is a description of the machine. An
evaluator sits with a scoring template and your submission, and awards points for
what is present, findable and specific. They cannot award points for what you
know and did not write down. They are not permitted to.

## Which is why the feedback never helped

You have probably had the short version back. Another bidder scored higher on
quality. Perhaps a number.

That sentence is true and it is useless, because it names the outcome and not the
line. Somewhere in your submission there was a paragraph that was general where
the template wanted specific, or a date that was six days later than someone
else's, or a claim you could have proved and did not think to.

Nobody is going to tell you which one. The buyer has no duty to, and the next
tender arrives with the same blank page.

## Before you say it is all price anyway

Sometimes it is, and where it is, nothing on this page will help you. In Latvia
between 2021 and 2023 the winner of 73% of procurements was picked on lowest
price alone. If that is your market, stop reading. You are right and I have
nothing to sell you.

Norway is the other end. Since 1 January 2024 Norwegian contracting authorities
have had to weight climate and environmental criteria at a minimum of 30%, and
during that first year around 75% of tenders carried it. A third of the score,
decided on documents, before anyone opens the commercial envelope.

Most of the tenders you bid sit between those two. The honest question is not
whether price matters. It is how much of the score was never about price, and
whether you have ever seen that part written down.

## What a document standard is

It is the set of things your firm asserts about itself, written once, properly,
in the form an evaluator scores.

Your capability. Your process. Your references, with the detail that makes them
count. Your environmental and quality evidence, stated as figures rather than
adjectives. The wording that survives being read by someone with a template and
nine other submissions to get through.

It is not a management system and you do not have to adopt anything. Nothing
changes in the workshop. The standard is a document, it belongs to you, and your
own people edit it.

The point is that the next tender starts from it instead of from nothing.

## What it takes from you

Four hours. Two conversations of about ninety minutes, and one read-through at
the end.

You supply what already exists. Past submissions, certificates, drawings,
whatever you sent last time. I do not need you to prepare anything, and I am not
going to ask you to fill in a questionnaire so that I can sell you the answers.

If you have sat through a pre-tender dialogue and come away feeling you had just
given away a day of free consulting, you know exactly why that sentence is in
here.

## €2,400

That is the full document standard, with one revision round included.

There is a smaller way in. €600 buys one document, done properly, and the full
€600 comes off the €2,400 if you go on. There is a larger one at €8,000 for the
whole identity system, and the €2,400 comes off that in turn. Walk the whole
ladder and you pay €8,000 in total, not €11,000.

For comparison, a Swedish bid consultancy publishes 15,000 kr excluding VAT for a
single draft of a single bid, delivered as a Word file. That is roughly what the
full standard costs here, and theirs expires the day that tender closes.

## We only bid three times a year, and only nearby

Good. That is the reader this is built for.

A firm bidding constantly can absorb a bad submission, because there is another
one next month. A firm bidding three times a year cannot. Each one carries a
third of your public work, and the one you lose on a paperwork date is not
replaced.

Bidding more is not the offer. The offer is that the few you do bid stop being
lost on the part nobody explained to you.

## Director's note

Mechanism first, because the reader knows the burden cold and has never been given
the cause. The page opens on a published ruling rather than a claim, since a
market at stage three rejects a seventh assertion but cannot reject a case number.
Register is authority throughout. The opacity belongs to the buyer and the page
never suggests the reader was careless.

The gradient runs from an unexplained loss to a document the reader's own people
hold. The high end is deliberately the flattest writing on the page, because it is
`[ENTAILED]` and not sourced, and dressing it up would make it invented.

**Two things to settle before this goes live.** The mechanism is `UNVERIFIED` as a
general claim about this reader and must be confirmed true before a manufacturer
sees it. And the Latvia paragraph disqualifies a real slice of the market on
purpose, which is the right call on the evidence and is still a commercial
decision that is yours, not mine.
