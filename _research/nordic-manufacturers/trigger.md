## Trigger — the EU Public Procurement Act proposal

**What it is:** The largest overhaul of EU procurement law in a decade. Published four days before this research.
**URLs:**

**Retrieved:** 2026-09-13

### Source material (extracted)

Substance, paraphrased:

- A single Public Procurement Act, in the form of a Regulation, replacing Directives 2014/23, 2014/24 and 2014/25. A Regulation applies directly with no national transposition, so the core rulebook converges across the 27.
- Best price-quality ratio becomes the standard award basis. Quality must carry at least 30% of the score, at least 50% for labour-intensive contracts, unless the buyer can justify otherwise. Lowest price no longer automatically wins.
- Quality is defined to include environmental and social factors, innovation, security, resilience — and European preference.
- European preference tools: restrict operators from third countries with no EU procurement agreement; require minimum EU-origin content; give covered suppliers an evaluation advantage; reject tenders where less than 50% of contract value is EU content. Member states able to exclude Chinese suppliers from certain tenders. Séjourné described it as radical simplification.
- Economic-security screening of bidder ownership, control, financing, and exposure to third-country disclosure laws.
- SME provisions: selection criteria limited to what is necessary and proportionate, explicitly curbing excessive turnover requirements and unjustified demands for prior public-sector experience; a published 'Needs Plan' pipeline at the start of each budget period; reusable digital business credentials; stronger division into lots.
- Commission estimates €650m annual administrative saving, of which €80m for buyers and €570m for participating companies.
- Scale figures quoted across sources: EU procurement around €2–2.5 trillion a year, 13.6–15% of GDP; Séjourné puts the contracts covered by the new rules at approximately €600 billion.
- Commission proposed a Regulation rather than a Directive despite objections from a majority of member states.

One quote, on what quality is now permitted to mean: "European preference is one of the things quality is allowed to mean."

## Trigger — green award criteria becoming mandatory, and the EPD as scoring currency

**What it is:** The shift from "environmental criteria if the buyer chooses" to "environmental criteria the buyer must weight," with dated national and EU instruments. This is the live documentation trigger in Norway today, and the EU-level one arrived in March 2026.

### Source material (extracted)

Substance, paraphrased:

- Norway's DFØ guidance explains how buyers apply the 30% rule and how to justify a deviation; deviations are described as rare in practice.
- Norwegian building code requires a climate gas account for new residential blocks and non-residential buildings, following NS 3720, covering A1–A4 plus B2 and B4 for specified components.
- Statsbygg reported a 27% emissions reduction since 2020 while growing activity, with materials the largest lever.
- Under Regulation (EU) 2026/718, contracting authorities must incorporate the four minimum environmental criteria into their specifications as mandatory requirements. Without the accrediting documentation the company is excluded from the tender. The analysis names SMEs in technology and energy as most exposed.
- The worked example given: a Spanish SME making energy storage systems, bidding on a EUR 2 million regional contract, which until now submitted standard technical specifications.
- EPD International names Sweden's Trafikverket and Boverket as green public procurement users of EPDs.
- Buyers in construction increasingly reference product-specific EN 15804+A2 EPDs as objective evidence rather than industry averages.

One quote, on the effect of an unverified claim: "Teams that send enviromental claims without third-party verification invite scoring penalties." (typo in original)

### Observations

- `[VERIFIED]` Norway is the sharpest case in the geography: a hard 30% minimum weighting, in force since 1 January 2024, with a reported ~75% adoption rate in year one. A Norwegian manufacturer without product-specific environmental documentation is losing a third of the available score before the technical evaluation begins. https://epd.guide/environmental-regulations-and-laws/norways-embodiedcarbon-rules-manufacturers-can-win
- `[VERIFIED]` Regulation (EU) 2026/718 is **exclusion, not scoring**. For clean-technology contracts the missing document does not cost points; it removes the bid. That is a different and much harder argument than a weighting argument, and it has been in force for under six months. https://cambioslegales.es/en/blog/environmental-requirements-public-procurement-clean-technologies-zero-emissions-2026
- `[VERIFIED]` The unverified-claim penalty is stated explicitly: a claim without third-party verification is worse than silence. That is directly relevant to any offer promising to help a manufacturer *say* things about themselves. Same epd.guide URL.
- `[INFERRED]` The pattern across Norway 2024, CPR January 2026, EU 2026/718 March 2026 and France August 2026 is that the artefact being demanded keeps changing while the underlying obligation — prove it with a verified document — stays the same. A manufacturer who solves each one as it arrives is permanently behind. A manufacturer with a system is not. That is a candidate problem mechanism and it is **not yet stated by anyone found in this research.** Rests on the four dated instruments above being independent of each other.
- `[VERIFIED]` **Contradiction to record.** Jorpex frames 2026 green procurement as changing "more narrowly than the headlines suggest," with mandatory criteria only in specific pockets. epd.guide and TenderMetric frame it as sustainability credentials directly affecting bid scores across the board. Both are defensible; they are describing mandatory scope versus practical prevalence. Record both, do not resolve.
- `[VERIFIED — AMENDED 2026-09-15]` **The January 2026 CPR row above was wrong, and it was wrong in the direction that flatters the offer.** Checked against Regulation (EU) 2024/3110 itself: the Regulation applies generally from 8 January 2026, but **Article 95(9)** applies the obligations on economic operators to a given product family only from **one year after adoption of an implementing act making a harmonised standard mandatory**, and **Article 92** brings penalties — including for environmental performance declarations — from **8 January 2027**, which is enforcement machinery rather than an obligation start. No product family has started that clock. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/3110/oj/eng
- `[VERIFIED — AMENDED 2026-09-15]` The error came from secondary sources — consultancy and EPD-vendor explainers — compressing "the Regulation applies" into "the obligation starts". **This file already flagged the same claim as contested** in the DPP section's recorded contradiction about steel. That flag was correct and the table row contradicted it. The table was the thing that was wrong.
- `[INFERRED]` Cambios Legales gates its full analysis behind a PRO plan. The public portion is a teaser. Treat its specifics on 2026/718 as directional and verify against the Official Journal text before any of it reaches a reader. **Primary source not retrieved.**

### Source material (extracted)

Substance, paraphrased:

- Typical gap of 18–24 months between adoption of a delegated act and its compliance deadline.
- A DPP is a structured, machine-readable record of identity, composition, durability and end-of-life data, reachable from a data carrier on the product. Explicitly not a PDF — a queryable record tied to a specific item or batch, pullable by a customs officer, a recycler or a buyer.
- ESPR Annex III allows delegated acts to require that information already mandated under other EU legislation — including elements of technical documentation and declarations of conformity — be made available through the DPP.
- The battery passport must carry material composition with geographic origin for conflict minerals, carbon footprint broken down by lifecycle stage, recycled-content data, and state-of-health metrics.
- Commission guidance reported as advising that carbon footprint and recycled-content shares be left blank on 18 February 2027.
- The 2025 Omnibus simplification package changed CSRD and CSDDD but left ESPR and DPP obligations unchanged.
- Infrastructure milestones affecting all categories: CEN/CENELEC standards, the EU registry, GS1 Sunrise.

One quote, on what not being named yet means: "Not being named yet means you have preparation time, not exemption."

### Observations

- `[VERIFIED]` ESPR Annex III lets the DPP absorb documentation the manufacturer already produces — technical files, declarations of conformity. The compliance burden is less about creating new facts than about having existing ones in a state where they can be handed over structured. That is a documents-system problem in its purest form. https://getreadycompliance.eu/eu-digital-product-passport-manufacturers/
- `[VERIFIED]` The registry went live 19 July 2026, eight weeks before this research, with no product obligation attached. The reader is in the window between the infrastructure existing and it binding. https://www.fiegenbaum.solutions/en/blog/digital-product-passport-from-european-regulation-to-global-standard
- `[INFERRED]` The commercially interesting fact is not the deadline but the 18–24 month lag: it means a buyer's *procurement* expectations move before the *legal* obligation does, because buyers write the coming standard into award criteria early. That would explain why manufacturers get asked for documents they are not yet legally required to hold. **Unverified — plausible, not evidenced. Do not use with a reader until confirmed.** Rests on combining the lag with the green-criteria pattern in the adjacent trigger file.
- `[VERIFIED]` **Contradiction to record.** Sources disagree on steel. wenture.io states GWP declarations for steel are already required from January 2026 under the revised CPR, with full DPP enforcement around 2027–2028. The Commission page lists steel DPP requirements at Q4 2026 for delegated act adoption. Circularise says ESPR begins with iron and steel from 2026. These are describing different instruments and different stages. Do not compress them into one date.
- `[VERIFIED — AMENDED 2026-09-15]` **An apparent contradiction raised elsewhere against this file does not survive reading it.** A later pass queried the 19 July 2026 ESPR row against sources stating no ESPR delegated act had been adopted as of August 2026, each carrying at least eighteen months' transition. The row already says **"Framework switch-on, not yet a product obligation"**, which is the distinction those sources are making. **This file was right and the challenge was mistaken.** Recorded so the same query is not raised a third time. The battery passport at 18 February 2027 remains the only fixed product-passport date.
- `[INFERRED]` Six of the sources on this topic are vendors selling DPP compliance software. The timeline is consistent across them, which is reassuring, but the urgency framing is not independent. The Commission page is the one to trust on dates: https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport_en

## Trigger — CSRD Omnibus I and the value chain cap

**What it is:** The EU sharply narrowing who must report sustainability data, and capping what large customers can demand from smaller suppliers. Important because it cuts *against* one of the obvious arguments for this offer, and a profile that misses it will build copy on a claim that has just been legislated away.
**URLs:**
- https://www.spectreco.com/blog/csrd-vsme-standard-2026-sme-suppliers-voluntary-reporting
- https://ngsfinland.fi/en/articles/csrd-omnibus-2026/
- https://commonwealthclimatelaw.org/publication/csrd-reporting-post-omnibus-i-what-directors-need-to-know-in-2026/
- https://www.getsunhat.com/blog/csrd-omnibus-packages-eu-compass
- https://tracera.com/csrd-value-chain-cap-manufacturers/
- https://www.re-flow.io/blog/csrd-omnibus-maritime-suppliers-value-chain-cap
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-13

---

### Dated sequence

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 29 January 2025 | "EU Compass to regain competitiveness" published |
| July 2025 | Commission issues official recommendation endorsing EFRAG's VSME |
| February 2026 | Omnibus I Directive adopted (cited as EU 2026/470) |
| March 2026 | Threshold update: approximately 31,000 small mid-caps exempted |
| 3 July 2026 | VSME adopted as EU law. Value chain cap binding from this date |
| ~November 2026 | Underlying delegated acts enter into force, approximately four months after adoption, after Parliament and Council scrutiny |
| FY beginning on or after 1 January 2027 | Value chain cap and VSME framework apply |

### Source material (extracted)

Substance, paraphrased:

- CSRD scope narrowed to companies with more than 1,000 employees **and** turnover above €450 million. In Finland, the original directive would have obligated approximately 800–1,000 companies; after reform, considerably fewer. Most mid-sized companies preparing for CSRD are now out of scope.
- Mandatory ESRS data points cut by roughly 70%, to approximately 320 core metrics.
- **Value chain cap:** a CSRD-obligated company may not request more sustainability data from a supplier with 1,000 or fewer employees than the VSME defines. Narrow exceptions for Scope 3 and climate-risk data.
- VSME Basic Module: approximately 50 datapoints.
- A "protected undertaking" is any company outside mandatory CSRD scope not exceeding 1,000 employees on average — covering most SMEs, mid-caps just under the threshold, and non-listed companies of any size below that headcount.
- Listed-SME Wave 3 no longer exists in its original form. 85% fewer companies required to report.
- Transposition into national law required within 12 months of entry into force; timelines will vary and some states may add stricter national requirements.
- Explicitly **not** capped: commercial procurement requirements. A customer can still require LCA data, product carbon footprints or EPDs as a condition of doing business. That is a commercial decision, not a CSRD obligation.
- Commonwealth Climate Law's caution: a legal cap on requests does not automatically translate into an exercisable right to refuse, for suppliers without the commercial leverage to say no.
- Pressure expected to build in H2 2026 as CSRD-obligated companies prepare first reports for FY2027.

One quote, on where the protection stops: "A shipowner can still require LCA data, product carbon footprints, or EPDs as a condition of doing business"

---

### Observations

- `[VERIFIED]` **This is the trigger that argues against the offer, and it belongs in the profile as an objection, not as a selling point.** A manufacturer under 1,000 employees has just been given a legal right to send back most sustainability questionnaires. https://www.spectreco.com/blog/csrd-vsme-standard-2026-sme-suppliers-voluntary-reporting
- `[VERIFIED]` The cap covers CSRD-driven requests only. It does not touch public procurement award criteria, and it does not touch commercial conditions of doing business. Both of those are the offer's actual ground. https://www.re-flow.io/blog/csrd-omnibus-maritime-suppliers-value-chain-cap
- `[INFERRED]` The gap between "the law says you can refuse" and "your largest customer asked" is the sharpest live tension in this market, and one source has already named it: a cap is not leverage. That is a candidate problem mechanism — the manufacturer's protection is legal and their exposure is commercial — and it removes blame cleanly. **Unverified as something any manufacturer has said in these terms.** Rests on the Commonwealth Climate Law caution.
- `[INFERRED]` There is a real risk the reader has read a headline saying the burden was cut by 85% and concluded the problem is over. Any opening that assumes the reader feels documentation pressure may be entering a conversation that ended in March 2026. Rests on the volume of "relief" framing across these sources.
- `[VERIFIED]` VSME gives a **named, official, 50-datapoint format** that did not exist before 3 July 2026. Aligning to a standard that now exists is a different and easier proposition than building one. Same Spectreco URL.
- `[VERIFIED]` **Contradiction to record.** getsunhat cites the Omnibus I Directive as EU 2026/470 adopted February 2026; Tracera says adopted February 2026; Spectreco dates the operative VSME adoption to 3 July 2026. These are different instruments in one package. **Primary sources not retrieved — verify against EUR-Lex before any date reaches a reader.**

## Trigger — defence and NATO procurement in north and northeast Europe

**What it is:** The fastest-growing procurement pipeline in the geography, and the one where documents, not price, decide who is allowed to compete.
**URLs:**

- https://nordicdefencesector.com/en/article/finland-purchases-thermal-imaging-add-on-sights-via-nspa
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-13

---

### Source material (extracted)

Substance, paraphrased:

- European defence spending reached €418 billion in 2025 and is still climbing. EU instruments pushing money toward joint buying and industrial capacity: EDIP, SAFE, the European Defence Fund.
- NATO common funding approximately €4.6 billion (2025), estimated €5.3 billion (2026). Member states' national defence budgets total well over $1 trillion.
- NSPA is NATO's largest procurement agency: maintenance and logistics, ammunition, fuel, spare parts, life-cycle services. It offers most opportunities to firms registered in its Source File. NCIA handles C4ISR, cybersecurity, software, secure communications, and qualifies suppliers through Basic Ordering Agreements. The NATO Investment Committee oversees infrastructure.
- Registration and prequalification are described as crucial to being recognised as an eligible bidder.
- Declaration of Eligibility: national certificate of technical, financial and professional capability. Tender-specific. Required especially in NSIP procurements above the Established Financial Limit of €1.6 million, and often in NCIA competitions and for the BOA. In Finland prepared by PVLOGLE and delivered via Finland's permanent representation to NATO.
- Personnel Security Clearance and Facility Security Clearance commonly required. Processes stated to take months.
- Finland's PUTU Act: no open procedure. Restricted and negotiated only. Suitability and references decide at the participation stage.
- The supply-chain path is described as often starting as a subcontractor inside a large system integrator's supply chain.
- NSPA competitions described as open to companies from all member states on equal terms, awarded on competition and best overall value, not nationality; NSPA emphasises competitive procurement across the full market rather than national return quotas.
- Worked example of the pipeline in practice: Finland's Ministry of Defence authorised procurement of TigIR-M20 thermal add-on sights through NSPA, supplied by Andres Industries AG, contract value over €24 million excluding VAT, part of the Taistelija programme.
- Finnish Ministry of Defence published a participation guide in June 2026.

One quote, on why nationality does not protect you: "awards are based on competition and best overall value — not the bidder's nationality"

---

### Observations

- `[VERIFIED]` The €24 million Finnish sight procurement went, through NSPA, to a **German** manufacturer. Proximity to the buyer is not an advantage here. A Nordic or Baltic manufacturer competing in its own backyard is competing against the whole alliance. https://nordicdefencesector.com/en/article/finland-purchases-thermal-imaging-add-on-sights-via-nspa
- `[VERIFIED]` PSC and FSC take months. DoE is tender-specific. Source File registration is a precondition. This is a pipeline where the documents must exist **before** the opportunity is visible — which inverts the usual bid-writing proposition entirely. https://www.haavi.ai/en/resources/nato-supplier-guide
- `[INFERRED]` This is the strongest available argument that the offer is a *pipeline* rather than a *service*: in defence, a document produced in response to a tender is already too late. Rests on the clearance timelines and the tender-specific nature of the DoE.
- `[INFERRED]` It is also the strongest available argument for the "partnerships" half of the offer, since the stated route in for smaller firms is subcontracting to a system integrator — which is a reputation problem with a private buyer, not a scoring problem with a public one. Rests on Haavi's description of the typical path.
- `[VERIFIED]` Thresholds for Finnish defence procurement, revised 1 January 2026: national €100,000 goods and services, €500,000 works; EU €432,000 and €5,404,000. Above-EU-threshold PUTU procurements publish to TED. https://www.haavi.ai/en/resources/defence-procurement
- `[INFERRED]` Everything in this file is Finland-weighted because Finland's post-accession guidance is the best documented in English. Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Sweden, Norway and Denmark each run their own national eligibility route and none was researched. **Significant geographic gap.**

### Source material (extracted)

- **Article 95(9):** the requirements and obligations of economic operators set out
  in Chapters I, II and III apply to a certain product family or product category
  only from one year after the date of adoption of an implementing act making a
  harmonised standard mandatory, unless a later application date is specified.
- Recital 110: obligations "should only apply mandatorily as of 12 months after
  the entry into force of harmonised technical specification".
- Recitals 108 and 112: designations, harmonised standards and European assessment
  documents adopted under Regulation (EU) No 305/2011 **maintain legal effects**;
  the Commission withdraws the old references as new harmonised technical
  specifications come into application. Transitional periods "should be long enough".
- Three standardisation requests adopted as of retrieval: precast normal,
  lightweight and autoclaved aerated concrete products (family 1); structural
  metallic products and ancillaries (family 20); cement, building limes and other
  hydraulic binders (family 15). Two of the three issued under the 2011 Regulation.
- Four more in preparation: reinforcing and prestressing steel and post-tensioning
  kits (16); doors, windows, shutters, gates and building hardware (2); thermal
  insulation and composite insulating kits (4); flat, profiled and block glass (30).

### Observations

- `[VERIFIED]` **A standardisation request is not a harmonised standard, and a
  harmonised standard is not an implementing act citing one.** Three stages
  separate where this sits today from where an obligation begins. **No product
  family has started its Article 95(9) clock.**
- `[VERIFIED]` **There is no CPR deadline to put in front of a
  construction-products manufacturer today, and none is imminent.** A segmentation
  argument built on the opposite was constructed on 2026-09-14 and withdrawn on
  2026-09-15.
- `[VERIFIED]` **This does not weaken the file's existing reading. It confirms it.**
  CPR joins the Act, ESPR and VSME as announced-and-not-yet-binding. The profile's
  conclusion that do-nothing is rational for most of this market stands, and now
  rests on four instruments rather than three.
- `[VERIFIED]` **Norway's 30 per cent weighting, in force 1 January 2024, remains
  the only live dated instrument reaching a general Nordic manufacturer.** Two
  years and eight months old, with a reported ~75 per cent adoption rate in year
  one. `[INFERRED]` That argues **Norway is the sharp segment** — on evidence,
  not on a forecast, and a narrower and better-founded cut than the
  construction-products one that was withdrawn.
- `[INFERRED]` **Method finding, and it is now a pattern rather than an incident.**
  Two of the three factual errors found across this research came from secondary
  sources restating a legal instrument. The third is the same shape: highly-ranked
  Norwegian guidance still describing the repealed 2001 and 2006 procurement
  regulations. **Anything that will be said to a client goes to the legal text.**
  This file already applies that rule to Cambios Legales and to getsunhat. It
  should have been applied to the CPR row.

### What to watch

`[VERIFIED]` The first implementing act citing a harmonised standard under this
Regulation is the event to track. **It starts a one-year clock and creates a real
dated obligation for a definable set of manufacturers** — precast concrete,
structural metallic products and cement first. That is the moment a compliance
argument becomes available without manufacturing it.
