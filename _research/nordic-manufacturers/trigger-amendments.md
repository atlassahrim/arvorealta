## New entry — Construction Products Regulation (EU) 2024/3110

**Retrieved 2026-09-15.** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/3110/oj/eng

`[VERIFIED]` Entered into force 7 January 2025 in part. **Most provisions apply
from 8 January 2026.** A small set — definitions, standards-development articles
and several annexes — applied from 7 January 2025.

`[VERIFIED]` **The manufacturer-facing obligations do not start on either date.**
**Article 95(9):** the requirements and obligations on economic operators in
Chapters I, II and III apply to a given product family or category only from
**one year after the date of adoption of an implementing act making a harmonised
standard mandatory**, unless a later date is specified.

`[VERIFIED]` **Article 92:** penalties — including enforcement of the
environmental performance declaration obligations — from **8 January 2027**. This
is enforcement machinery and not the start of an obligation.

`[VERIFIED]` As of 2026-09-15 the Commission had adopted **three standardisation
requests**: precast normal, lightweight and autoclaved aerated concrete products
(family 1); structural metallic products and ancillaries (family 20); and cement,
building limes and other hydraulic binders (family 15). Two of the three were
issued under the 2011 Regulation. **Four more are in preparation:** reinforcing
and prestressing steel and post-tensioning kits (family 16); doors, windows,
shutters, gates and building hardware (family 2); thermal insulation products and
composite insulating kits (family 4); flat, profiled and block glass (family 30).

`[VERIFIED]` A standardisation request is not a harmonised standard, and a
harmonised standard is not an implementing act citing it. **No product family has
started its Article 95(9) clock.** The first CPR working plan covers 2026 to 2029.

`[VERIFIED]` The Commission was obliged to publish that first working plan no
later than 8 January 2026.

### Why this entry exists

`[VERIFIED]` On 2026-09-14 this regulation was recorded — from secondary sources,
consultancy and EPD-vendor explainers — as making GWP a mandatory Declaration of
Performance entry for priority categories from 8 January 2026, and a live
obligation for a construction-products manufacturer. **That was wrong.** Those
sources compressed "the Regulation applies" into "the obligation starts". The
error was caught by reading the Regulation's own articles on 2026-09-15.

`[INFERRED]` **Method finding worth keeping.** Two of the three factual errors
found across this research so far came from secondary sources restating a legal
instrument, and the third is the same shape — highly-ranked Norwegian guidance
still describing the repealed 2001 and 2006 procurement regulations. **Anything
that will be said to a client goes to the legal text.**

## Replacement entry — the September 2026 instrument

`[VERIFIED]` Retrieved 2026-09-15. The Public Procurement Act presented on
**9 September 2026** is a proposed **single Regulation intended to replace
Directives 2014/24/EU (public sector), 2014/25/EU (utilities) and 2014/23/EU
(concessions)** — not an amendment to them.

`[VERIFIED]` A Regulation applies directly in all member states with no national
transposition, which removes the discretion member states currently hold over how
they apply procurement rules. That is a materially different instrument from what
"an Act" amending directives would be, and it changes how much national variation
survives.

`[VERIFIED]` Best price-quality ratio becomes the standard award method. Quality
criteria at **minimum 30 per cent**, **50 per cent for labour-intensive
contracts**, subject to a comply-or-explain mechanism. Criteria may cover
environmental performance, innovation, security, resilience and social
considerations.

`[VERIFIED]` **It is a proposal.** It must be negotiated and approved by the
European Parliament and the Council before it can enter into force. Nothing
downstream may describe it as law.

`[INFERRED]` It reaches **Norway and Iceland only through incorporation into the
EEA agreement, on a separate and later timetable.** Both are EEA, not EU. The
research to date treats the five-country geography as one regulatory unit and it
is not one. Timing unverified.

## Open, and flagged for the next pass

`[VERIFIED]` **The ESPR dating in `trigger.md` is contested.** `trigger.md`
records 19 July 2026 as ESPR reaching full application with an EU Central DPP
Registry going live. Sources retrieved 2026-09-14 state that as of August 2026
**no ESPR delegated act had been adopted**, each carrying a transition period of
at least eighteen months, with indicative timings of iron and steel from 2026,
textiles, tyres and aluminium from 2027, furniture from 2028, ICT and mattresses
from 2029.

`[INFERRED]` The likely reconciliation is that the framework regulation applying
and a product category actually being obliged are different events — the same
distinction that caused the CPR error above. **Unconfirmed.** Until it is
settled, the battery passport at **18 February 2027** is the only product-passport
date safe to state to anyone.
