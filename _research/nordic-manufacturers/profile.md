### 2. The trigger

- **What changed recently that made this live:** `[VERIFIED]` Five dated instruments, all in `trigger.md`:
  - 9 September 2026 — Commission presents the Public Procurement Act. Quality must carry at least 30% of the score, 50% for labour-intensive contracts. `[AMENDED 2026-09-15]` It is a **proposed single Regulation replacing Directives 2014/24, 2014/25 and 2014/23** — not an amendment to them — and it has passed **neither Parliament nor Council**. A Regulation applies directly with no national transposition, which removes the member-state discretion currently shaping how each Nordic country runs procurement. `[INFERRED]` It reaches Norway and Iceland only through EEA incorporation, on a separate and later timetable.
  - 19 July 2026 — ESPR reaches full application; EU Central DPP Registry goes live.
  - 3 July 2026 — VSME adopted as EU law; value chain cap binding.
  - 20 March 2026 — Regulation (EU) 2026/718: clean-technology public contracts must accredit carbon footprint, durability, recyclability, efficiency. Missing documentation excludes rather than deducts.
  - 1 January 2024 — Norway: climate and environmental criteria weighted at minimum 30%, reportedly applied in roughly 75% of tenders during 2024.
  - `[AMENDED 2026-09-15 — sixth instrument, and it does not do what it first appeared to]` 8 January 2026 — Construction Products Regulation (EU) 2024/3110 applies generally. **It obliges no manufacturer to declare GWP on that date.** Article 95(9) reaches a product family only one year after an implementing act makes a harmonised standard mandatory; Article 92 brings penalties from 8 January 2027. Three standardisation requests exist, no implementing act. **No family has started its clock.** See `trigger.md`.
- **How long has it been live:** `[VERIFIED]` Norwegian manufacturer: two years and eight months. Clean-tech manufacturer: under six months. Everyone else: announced, not binding. `[AMENDED 2026-09-15]` CPR belongs in the third group, not the first. **Norway remains the only live dated instrument reaching a general Nordic manufacturer** — which makes Norway the sharp segment, on evidence rather than on a forecast.
- **What happens if they do nothing for another cycle:** `[VERIFIED]` Norway and the 2026/718 categories: scoring loss or exclusion, now. `[INFERRED]` Everywhere else: nothing visible, which is the harder problem. The Act does not apply before roughly 2029–2030.
- **Is there an external deadline, and whose is it:** `[VERIFIED]` 18 February 2027 — the battery passport, for EV, industrial (>2 kWh) and light-means-of-transport batteries only. For any other product category the deadline belongs to the buyer, not the law. `[INFERRED]` Buyer expectation moves ahead of legal obligation because delegated acts run 18–24 months ahead of compliance dates. **Flagged in `trigger.md` as plausible and unevidenced. Still unevidenced after the second pass.**
