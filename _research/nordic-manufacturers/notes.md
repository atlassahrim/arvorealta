## The claim inventory and the sophistication reading

### What the market has heard, repeatedly

`[RECOVERED]` The lost file counted **ten claims recurring three or more times
across sellers.** Six were named in `profile.md` and the other four are gone:

1. Quality criteria matter more than you think
2. Make it easy for the evaluator to find the evidence
3. You are losing points you do not know you are losing
4. Sustainability documentation is a scoring asset
5. The admin burden is crushing SMEs
6. We will find you the tenders you are missing

**The list is incomplete and must be treated as incomplete.** Four of ten are
missing and any of them could be the one that matters.

### Fresh confirmation, 2026-09-16

`[VERIFIED]` Claims 1, 2 and 3 were all re-found today in a single Swedish
competitor's page, compressed into two sentences:

> *answer exactly what is asked, prove every claim, and write against the scoring template*

> "En medioker text kan kosta mer poäng än ett högre pris."
> *A mediocre text can cost more points than a higher price.*

https://anbudspartner.se/anbudsskrivning · retrieved 2026-09-16 · `competitor.md`

`[VERIFIED]` Claim 5 re-found today on the English side, as a pricing rationale

https://tsaksconsulting.com/blog/how-much-does-a-professional-bid-writing-service-cost-in-the-uk-2026-and-2027-fee-guide/ · retrieved 2026-09-16

`[VERIFIED]` **These claims are alive in the market today, in two countries, in

### The mechanism reading

`[RECOVERED]` Three mechanisms exist market-wide, each locked to a niche:

| Mechanism | Owned by | Niche |
|---|---|---|
| The default-data penalty | EPD-category vendors | environmental declarations |
| The bid-cost arithmetic | Tendergate | bid economics |
| Restricted-procedure gating | Haavi | Finnish defence |

`[VERIFIED]` A fourth was found today and it is the closest to Arvorealta's own:
Anbudspartner's *answer exactly, prove every claim, write against the scoring
template*. It is a genuine problem mechanism — it explains **why** bids lose
points rather than asserting that they do. https://anbudspartner.se/anbudsskrivning

`[VERIFIED]` **Nobody in six countries was found explaining why the same
documentation is rebuilt from scratch every cycle.** The bid writers explain how
to write a bid. The software explains how to store answers. Neither explains the
cause of the repetition. That is the unoccupied mechanism slot.

### The reading

`[INFERRED]` **Stage three on the claim, stage one or two on the mechanism.** The
market has heard *we help you win tenders* so often that it has stopped hearing
it, while the causal account of why documentation work repeats has not been
published by anyone located in this research. Rests on the claim inventory above
and on the nine-firm Swedish field in `competitor.md`, all selling the same unit.

`[INFERRED]` This is the gap ladder rung 09 describes as the place the opening
usually lives. **It is also the most fragile finding in this pack**, because it
is an argument from absence, and absence is what a search returns when the words
are wrong. Two Estonian and Latvian vendor searches remain unrun.

## Market structure

### Competition is falling, and it cuts against urgency

`[RECOVERED]` Average bidders per EU procedure fell from **5.7 in 2011 to 3.2 in
2021.**

`[RECOVERED]` The consequence is uncomfortable and was already recorded in
`profile.md`: whoever stays in faces **fewer** rivals, not more. **A copy
argument built on rising competition is contradicted by the market's own data.**

`[VERIFIED]` Swedish figures from the same period point the same way. Almega
gives 4.8 average bids per advertised procurement overall, 5.9 in services, down
from 7.1; in service procurements **19% attracted exactly one bid and 20%
attracted two, so 39% drew no more than two.**
https://www.almega.se/app/uploads/2024/10/Rapport-Offentlig-upphandling-ur-tjansteforetagens-perspektiv.pdf · via `voice.md`

`[INFERRED]` Thin fields are an argument for *entering*, not for *polishing*.
If 39% of Swedish service procurements draw two bids or fewer, the binding
constraint for a withdrawn manufacturer is the decision to bid at all, and the
document standard is what makes that decision cheap to repeat. **That is a
different argument from "your documents are losing you points" and the two
should not be run in the same page.**

### What do nothing looks like, and what it costs

`[RECOVERED / VERIFIED via voice.md]` Withdrawal, stated with pride, in the first
person plural: *we manage without them; we are paid in full by private clients
without the hassle; we have stopped with public procurement entirely.* Almega
free-text, Swedish service companies — **not manufacturers.**

`[VERIFIED via voice.md]` Företagarna gives the aggregate for Swedish small
business: **32% take part, 48% say it has become more awkward in recent years,
17% abstain because it is too complicated and takes too long.**

`[VERIFIED]` England, independently, gives a matching figure for a different
population. The **UK Supply Report 2026**, Critical Supply Group with MAP UK &
International, Made in Britain, the All-Party Parliamentary Manufacturing Group,
CIPS, CBI and FSB, surveying **nearly 200 businesses** in critical sectors:
**67% identified procurement and tendering complexity as the biggest obstacle**,
64% limited visibility of opportunities, 39% insufficient engagement from buyers,
and 74% believe they could increase their contribution.
https://www.operationsengineer.org.uk/content/news/survey-highlights-untapped-capability-in-supply-chains · retrieved 2026-09-16

`[VERIFIED]` **This is the first hard number in the whole research pack drawn
from a manufacturing population rather than a services one.** Every earlier
aggregate — Almega, Företagarna — carried a non-manufacturer caveat. The 67% does
not. It is English rather than Nordic, and that limit travels with it.

`[INFERRED]` *Do nothing* in this market is not passive and does not feel like
loss. It is an articulated, defended position with a pride attached to it. Ladder
rung 07 names do-nothing as the market leader in nearly every category; here it
is the market leader **and** the reader's preferred self-description. Copy that
implies the reader has been neglecting something is arguing with a decision they
made on purpose.

## Contradictions, recorded and left open

The skill's instruction is to record and not resolve. Each of these is a place
where two sources disagree and the disagreement is itself the finding.

### 1. Bid counts that cannot share a sentence

`[VERIFIED]` Almega: **4.8** average bids per advertised Swedish procurement,
2022 data. TED-based EU figures: **3.2** average bidders per tender, 2021.
Different populations, different counting rules, different years. Flagged in
`voice.md` and carried here as instructed. **Not a conflict, but they cannot
appear in the same sentence.**

### 2. The study that contradicts the offer's premise

`[RECOVERED]` **Stake (2017)** found that switching Swedish awards from lowest
price to most-economically-advantageous did **not** raise SME participation or
success rates.

`[VERIFIED]` This directly challenges the reasoning in `trigger.md` that the EU's
proposed 30% and 50% quality minimums open a window for a documents offer. If a
quality-weighting shift did nothing for SMEs in Sweden once already, the
assumption that the next one will is unsupported.

`[VERIFIED]` **The original citation is lost with `notes.md`.** Author surname,
year and finding survive; journal, title and URL do not. **Re-source before
relying on it, and do not cite it to a client in this state.**

### 3. Latvia points the opposite way from the EU narrative

`[VERIFIED via voice.md]` In 2021–2023 the winner of **73%** of Latvian
procurements was selected solely on lowest price. FICIL also states its fairness
and transparency concerns in Latvia are *much more pronounced* than in Estonia
and Lithuania.

`[INFERRED]` Where price alone decides three awards in four, a documents-and-
quality offer has a much smaller addressable slice than the EU quality-floor
story implies — and the reform that would change it applies 2029–2030 at the
earliest. **Latvia may be the weakest of the six markets for this offer and is
currently being addressed as though it were one of the strong ones.**

### 4. England has legislated half of the offer's premise already

`[VERIFIED]` The Procurement Act 2023 went live on **24 February 2025**. Its
Central Digital Platform lets a supplier store core business details once and
reuse them across bids, free of charge: *"Find a Tender is free to use for
everyone. There are no charges and you should not pay to view opportunities in
the public sector."*
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/procurement-act-2023-short-guides/the-procurement-act-2023-a-short-guide-for-suppliers-html · retrieved 2026-09-16

`[INFERRED]` **The state has taken the administrative half of "enter it once" and
made it free.** What it has not taken is the technical and quality narrative — the
method statements, the evidence, the sentences an evaluator scores — which is the
half Arvorealta actually sells. In England the offer must be positioned against a
free government platform that already solves the part everyone thinks of first.
Unverified whether English manufacturers experience the platform as solving it.

### 5. The complainants know exactly why they lost

`[VERIFIED via voice.md]` Every Danish, Norwegian and Finnish complainant located
across two passes **believes they know precisely why they lost and argues it at
length.** The profile's most expensive inference is that the reader has been
losing for years without finding out why.

`[VERIFIED]` Either that inference is wrong, or complainants are a self-selecting
minority who complain *because* they know, and the silent majority is the reader.
**Desk research cannot separate these two and a third pass will not either.**

`[CLIENT]` **Resolved 2026-09-16, by the client rather than by research.** Atlas
Sahrim: *"They usually do not know why they lose."* The second reading is the
right one. **The complaint archives are the wrong population for section 8** —
every objection drawn from them describes an unusually informed supplier. See
`voice.md`, client answers.

### 6. EPD registration fees that cannot both be true

`[VERIFIED]` One source gives *"EUR 1,000 for the first EPD... then a volume

## The method finding worth keeping

`[VERIFIED]` Carried forward from `trigger-amendments.md`, now merged into
`trigger.md`: of the factual errors found across this research so far, **two came
from secondary sources restating a legal instrument**, and a third was
highly-ranked Norwegian guidance still describing repealed 2001 and 2006
procurement regulations. The Construction Products Regulation error — consultancy
explainers compressing *"the Regulation applies"* into *"the obligation starts"* —
was caught only by reading the Regulation's own articles.

`[VERIFIED]` **Anything that will be said to a client goes to the legal text.**
This applies with particular force to the EU Public Procurement Act proposal of
9 September 2026, which is **a proposal** and which nothing downstream may
describe as law.
