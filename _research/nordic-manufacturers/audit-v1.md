# Audit v1 — nordic-manufacturers / draft-v1

**Run:** 2026-09-16 · **Against:** `profile.md` (v2.1, 2026-09-15) and `voice.md`
**Rungs verifiable:** 09 to 00. `profile.md` is present, so 09 through 06 are checked against evidence rather than assumed.

## 1. The break

**Rung 07 — positioning. *Do nothing* is never engaged.**

The profile names it as the winner, verified, and counts the reasons:

> **Which of these is actually winning right now:** `[VERIFIED]` **Do nothing, and it is now supplier-stated rather than inferred.** Almega respondents say they have stopped entirely; Företagarna puts participation at 32%; the field is thinning; no competitor exists in the geography. Four independent reasons for inaction this quarter.

The handoff turns that into an instruction the draft was given and did not take:

> **Opening must:** enter on the reader's competence, not their failure. **Engage *do nothing* explicitly and treat it as rational, because on the evidence it is** — and because the suppliers who chose it say so with pride, not regret.

Nine sections, and none of them speaks to a reader who has stopped bidding or is waiting for the next cycle. The closest is section 8, and it answers a different objection:

> ## We only bid three times a year, and only nearby
>
> Good. That is the reader this is built for.

That engages **low frequency**, not **withdrawal**. The profile keeps them apart: do nothing "looks like withdrawal, stated with pride, in the first person plural, repeatedly: we manage without them, we are paid in full by private clients without the hassle, we have stopped with public-procurement business entirely." The section then concedes the ground it needed: "Bidding more is not the offer."

Delay is unengaged too, and the profile calls it "`[VERIFIED]` Rational for most readers. The Act applies from roughly 2029–2030. Waiting costs nothing outside Norway and the 2026/718 clean-tech categories."

The axis itself is fine. €2,400 names a real dimension with a real player at the far end: a durable standard the reader's own people edit, against a Swedish consultancy's single-use bid draft that "expires the day that tender closes." But that axis only does work on a reader already choosing between vendors, and the profile's finding is that nobody in this market is. The page positions against a competitor while the thing actually taking the reader's decision is inertia, which is the exact failure rung 07 describes: "Most copy never engages it, because writers position against firms rather than against inertia."

This clears the bar on both tests. The absence is provable by exhaustion across every section, and it changes whether the reader acts rather than how the page reads: the modal reader agrees with every line and does nothing, because nothing on the page argues with that.

Rungs 09, 08 and 06 hold. Mechanism-first is the correct shape for a market at stage three on the claim and low on the mechanism; the page opens on a case number rather than a seventh assertion; it claims no deadline, reuses none of the ten dead claims, does not lead with environmental documentation, and says "It is not a management system" where the profile's most dangerous objection sits. Rung 06 is the strongest work here: effort is named as four hours and two conversations, with "I am not going to ask you to fill in a questionnaire so that I can sell you the answers" answering Almega's free-consulting line directly, and no money-back guarantee anywhere.

## 2. The fix

Engage withdrawal where the copy causes it, which is immediately after the price, and treat it as the sound commercial decision the evidence says it is. Concede it fully before complicating it.

Then use the one number in the research that flips it without inventing urgency: average bidders per EU procedure fell from 5.7 in 2011 to 3.2 in 2021. The field is thinning for exactly the reason the reader would withdraw, which means the contest gets easier to win as it gets harder to enter. That argues against inertia without claiming a deadline the handoff forbids.

Substitute this for section 8. Keep its three-bids-a-year arithmetic, which works, and put it after the concession rather than in place of it.

## 3. The rewrite

Replacing **"We only bid three times a year, and only nearby"** in full.

---

## We manage without them

A Swedish firm said that to a trade body survey, and said it with pride rather than regret. We manage without them. We get paid in full by private clients, completely without hassle.

That is what this page is competing with, and it is winning. Företagarna puts small-company participation at 32%. Of the firms staying out, 17% say it is too complicated and takes too long.

They are not wrong. Public work argues about scope, pays on its own schedule, and costs you a week of unpaid writing before anyone looks at your price. A firm that walked away and filled the capacity with private customers made a sound decision, and nothing here says otherwise.

One number complicates it.

The average number of bidders per EU procedure fell from 5.7 in 2011 to 3.2 in 2021. The field is thinning, and it is thinning for the reason above. Firms are leaving because the paperwork became unreasonable, not because they stopped being able to do the work.

So the contest is getting easier to win at the same time as it is getting harder to enter. Everyone who withdraws hands their share to whoever can still produce the documents.

You bid three times a year. That will not change and it does not need to. A firm bidding constantly can absorb a bad submission, because there is another one next month. Yours carries a third of your public work, and the one you lose on a paperwork date is not replaced.

Bidding more is not the offer. The offer is that the few you do bid stop being lost on the part nobody explained to you.

Before you spend €2,400 finding that out, spend nothing.

## 4. Notes

Preferences and lower-rung items. None of these is the reason the page would not convert.

- **"That is two countries, two industries, and one finding."** It is not a finding. Mesta's is a complainant's allegation to KOFA, and the profile marks it exactly: "That is not the same claim and does not confirm it... **Still a candidate, not a finding.**" The draft attributes it correctly one sentence earlier ("Mesta argued to KOFA") and then upgrades it. Change to *the same complaint* and nothing else needs touching. This is a truth item, so fix it regardless of the break.

- **"Which is why the feedback never helped" crosses a line mid-section.** Buyer silence is verified: P. Olesen's own words are that "nothing appears in the award about the criteria for assessment." Reader ignorance is not, and `voice.md` is blunt about it: "No source in either pass contains a supplier admitting they do not know why they lose. The Danish and Norwegian complainants all believe they know exactly why they lost, and say so at length." Keep the section on what the buyer withheld. Drop the assertions about what the reader does and does not know. The headline survives on the buyer-silence reading; that section is where it stops surviving.

- **The gate's check 5 passes on a relabel.** The draft's gate calls PlugIt Finland Oy a "Finnish manufacturer." `profile.md` labels it `[VERIFIED, product company]` and states in bold: "**None is from a 50–250 person Nordic manufacturer of the kind section 1 describes.**" On the profile's own wording, check 5 fails and `/plan` should have stopped. The draft half-concedes this ("thinner than the count suggests") rather than naming it.

- **The gate quarantines its best evidence for no reason.** It calls the Danish lines "out of the brief's geography." The profile's geography is Finland, Sweden, Norway and Denmark. Denmark is in scope, and P. Olesen is the strongest thing on the page.

- **Two cited source files are not in the repo.** `profile.md` reads from `notes.md` and `competitor.md` throughout; neither exists in `_research/nordic-manufacturers/`. The 15,000 kr consultancy price and the 5.7-to-3.2 bidder figure both trace there, and the rewrite above leans on the second. Verify both before this goes live, or cut the comparison.

- **"Around 75% of tenders carried it"** is rounded, and `trigger.md` hedges it further ("reported to have risen to approximately 75%"). The draft preserves the hedge, which is honest. If the underlying figure is precise, use it; rounding costs evidentiary value at rung 02.

- **The high position is flat on purpose, and that is the right call.** Section 6 of the profile is `[MISSING]` after two passes and five search routes. Writing a ceiling that is not there would invent it. The consequence is a gentle slope, and after 07 this is the weakest rung on the page. It is fixed by research, not by writing.

- **The hero repeats verbatim at the close**, which matches the house pattern in `CLAUDE.md`. If the hero changes, both instances change together.
