# Where to meet the buyer first

**Written 6 November 2026.** This answers one question for three
solutions: where is the single point of contact, and the venue, with the
most impact? And where should ten hours a week go first?

**Tags.** Every claim carries one tag:

- `VERIFIED`: checked at source, with the source named.
- `INFERRED`: reasoned from verified material.
- `GUESS`: a working hypothesis to test.
- `MISSING`: not found.
- `CLIENT`: the owner's own statement or decision.

**Seller language.** Where a figure comes from an agency selling the
thing, the text says so.

## The answer

Spend the first two weeks on brand. The buyer reaches the identity
question when the website is about to be built. At that point they ask
someone they trust: *who can do our brand?* The person they ask is most
often the small web or marketing studio building the site, so **that
studio is the venue**.

The plan is to become the identity partner for three to five studios in
Tallinn, Helsinki and Oslo. You offer a fixed €8,000 scope and a clean
spec they can build from. You pay a 10 per cent fee for any introduction
that signs.

It scores highest of the ten venues for four reasons:

- It borrows trust you do not yet have in these markets.
- It costs nothing but time.
- One partner can send work more than once.
- It also carries video.

**Fix the mail before sending anything.** `arvorealta.com` has no DMARC
record, and no DKIM key at Google's default selector `VERIFIED`. The four
reviews sent in October went out with PDF attachments from that domain.
So zero replies may mean they were never seen `INFERRED`.

What to stop or skip:

- **Pause the tender reviews for 14 days.**
- **Sell video only as part of an identity launch.**
- **Do not buy** directory listings, ads or conference tickets.
- **Do not bid** on public identity tenders yet.

## 1. The buyer

### The buyer for brand, the priority

**Who.** The founder or managing director of a company of 10 to 100
people in Norway, Finland, Sweden or Estonia. The company works in English
or tolerates it, and something has just changed: its name, its owner, its
market, or its website `GUESS`.

**Who pays.** The same person. Below about €10,000 they can sign alone
`GUESS`.

The €10,000 second-signature line comes from this repo's own pricing note
about manufacturers `CLIENT`. It has not been checked for this buyer.

**What it costs elsewhere.** European bands put a full identity with a
guide at €2,500 to €8,000 at a boutique studio. They put €12,000 to
€40,000 at a mid-size agency. Both figures are seller language, from
studios selling identity ([nudstudio], [viralistic]).

€8,000 sits at the top of the boutique band and well under an agency
`INFERRED`. That is a comfortable place to sell from, and room for a
studio partner's fee.

### Shared buyers

- **Brand and documents share a role.** The reader in
  `nordic-manufacturers/profile.md` is the MD of a manufacturer of about
  90 people. The document ladder's top rung is the same €8,000 identity
  system `CLIENT`. One person can therefore enter at either end. So far
  that buyer has answered zero of four approaches `VERIFIED`
  (`approach-log.md`).
- **Brand and video share the buyer and the moment.** A new identity has
  to be launched, and the launch is social video `INFERRED`.

### The path, for brand

| Stage | What happens | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | A rename, merger, new owner, funding round, new market, or website rebuild. The website rebuild is where an unsettled brand becomes a blocker. | `INFERRED` |
| Searching | They ask people they trust before they search. In Hinge's professional-services research, referrals are the leading route to a firm. One older study puts referrals at 71 per cent of discovery and search at 11 per cent. A later one says fewer than 60 per cent ask, with search at about one in five. | `VERIFIED`, secondary write-ups of the Hinge studies |
| Comparing | Two to four portfolios, and perhaps one reference call. | `GUESS`, vendor guides only |
| Deciding | The founder alone, on craft and trust. | `GUESS` |
| Buying | A fixed-scope proposal, then a deposit. | `INFERRED` |

**The one place.** Meet them at the searching stage, inside the
conversation with the studio that is about to build their site.

That studio already has the founder's trust and a deadline that depends on
the brand. It also sees the problem before the founder has started looking
`INFERRED`.

## 2. The scoring

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5, and 5 is always the best:

- **Proximity:** how close the venue is to the moment of pain.
- **Reach:** whether you reach the person who decides and pays.
- **Cost:** cost per real conversation; 5 is the cheapest.
- **Cycle:** how quickly a conversation can become a sale; 5 is the
  shortest.
- **Ladder:** how well it fits the price ladder.
- **Repeat:** whether it can be repeated.
- **Evidence:** how much evidence supports it.

The scores are judgement built on the tagged facts, not measurements
`INFERRED`.

- **B's weakness is the timing and the approach.** Nobody knows whether a
  renamed company has already bought its identity before it registers the

  Sweden and Estonia `CLIENT`. If the market is negotiable for the first
  €8,000, C goes first. That is fact 3 below.
- **E is the closest to the pain on the list.** A public body publishes

  for references, so each conversation costs a full bid.
- **D is small.** Estonia had 58 funding rounds across 53 startups in 2025
  `VERIFIED` ([funding.tech.eu]). Most are seed or pre-seed, where €8,000
  is a large share of the money `INFERRED`.

The budget is ten hours a week, so about 18 hours across the two weeks.
The money is €0. The fee to a partner is paid only out of a signed job.

### The message

**Who it goes to.** The founder of the studio.

**Where it is sent.** Their own email address, from yours, once DKIM and
DMARC pass. If only a contact form exists, use the same text there.

**What is attached.** Nothing. The link does the work. A stranger's
attachment is the thing a cautious reader does not open. This repo learned
that once already with `DR-001-NO.pdf` `VERIFIED` (`CLAUDE.md`). It also
costs deliverability `INFERRED`.

Fill in the bracketed parts yourself:

> **Subject:** When a client needs a brand before the site
>
> Hi [first name],
>
> [One sentence about one real project of theirs: what it does well.]
>
> I'm Atlas Sahrim. I design identity systems: one mark, three colours in
> a fixed proportion, one typeface, and rules a web team can build from.
> The scope is fixed at €8,000 and takes [weeks]. Seventeen are at
> arvorealta.com/brand/
>
> Sometimes a client's site cannot start because the brand is not
> settled. When that happens I can take the identity off your plate and
> hand you a clean spec to build on. The client and the site stay yours,
> and any introduction that signs earns 10% of the fee.
>
> Would 20 minutes next week be useful?
>
> Atlas

**Your decisions before sending.** The 10 per cent fee and the number of
weeks are yours to set `CLIENT`. If a studio would rather white-label the
work, decide your floor price in advance.

**On the call, ask three things.** How often does a client arrive without
a settled brand? What do you do then? Who at the client signs for it?

### How to read the result

- **Proof the mail works:** DKIM and DMARC read `pass` on 9 November. If
  they do not, nothing else in this plan is measurable.
- **Success:** by 22 November, 30 delivered, three or more replies, and at
  least one call booked.
- **Strong success:** one studio names a current client who needs a brand.
- **Failure:** fewer than two replies from 30 delivered. In that case,
  move the next 14 days to brand B. Send ten notes a week to renamed
  limited companies with five or more staff, taken from the Brønnøysund
  feed, and measure them the same way.

## 4. What not to do, and why

- **Do not send more tender reviews now.** Four went out with zero replies
  `VERIFIED`. Each one costs hours of scoring, and brand is the priority
  `CLIENT`.
- **Do not buy Clutch placement, ads or conference tickets.** €50 a month
  buys none of them at a useful level. A directory puts an €8,000
  identity in a price comparison against €2,500 studios `INFERRED`.
- **Do not bid on public identity tenders yet.** Each bid is a full
  document in Norwegian or Finnish, with references from that market that
  you do not yet have `INFERRED`. Revisit after the first Nordic client.
- **Do not make LinkedIn posting the plan.** It is the slowest venue on
  the list and spends the ten hours on reach rather than on conversations
  `INFERRED`.
- **Do not sell video on its own before it has a price.** A conversation
  that ends in "what does it cost?" with no answer is a lost conversation.

## 5. Risks

1. **Deliverability is unproven.** The missing records are a fact
   `VERIFIED`. Whether they caused the silence is not `MISSING`.
2. **There is a proof gap.** Most of the seventeen marks are for Brazilian
   clients. A studio judges the craft first, but a Nordic end client may
   ask for local proof `INFERRED`.
3. **Delivery competes with selling.** At ten hours a week, one signed
   identity stops outreach for weeks. The pipeline will be lumpy, so keep
   one hour a week of follow-ups running during any job.
4. **Partners can stall.** A studio may say yes and never send anyone.
   Five partners at a low rate beat one enthusiastic partner `GUESS`.
5. **The price could undercut the partner.** If a studio sells identity
   itself, even badly, the offer competes with it. That is why the list
   filters those studios out.
6. **The language is limited.** Studios will take English. A Norwegian or
   Finnish end client may want the brand guidelines written in their
   language `INFERRED`.

## 6. The three facts that would most change this

1. **Did the October emails reach the inbox?** If they did, and still drew
   nothing, then cold mail to strangers is the weak point. That lowers the
   expected result for the partner emails too, and raises the value of any
   warm route. Checkable on 9 November with test sends.
2. **Do you have any warm network in the four countries?** Former
   colleagues, a studio you know, a co-working space. **One warm Nordic
   introduction beats every cold venue on these tables.**
3. **Is the market negotiable for the first €8,000?** If an introduction
   from a Brazilian client to a company that can pay in euros counts, C
   wins brand and goes first.

## 7. Five questions for a real buyer

Ask a founder or MD in Norway, Finland, Sweden or Estonia who bought an
identity in the last two years:

1. What happened in the month before you decided the brand had to change?
2. Who was the first person you asked, and what did they tell you?
3. How did you find each studio you considered, and how many did you look
   at?
4. What made you drop the ones you dropped?
5. What did it cost, who approved the spend, and how many weeks passed
   from the first call to the signature?
