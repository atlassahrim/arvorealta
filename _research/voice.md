## Voice — Almega member survey, Swedish service companies

**What it is:** A named trade association's survey of its own member companies about public procurement, containing dozens of unedited free-text answers from suppliers. This is the richest seam of buyer-side language located anywhere in this research.
**URL:** https://www.almega.se/2024/10/rapport-offentlig-upphandling-ur-tjansteforetagens-perspektiv/

**Retrieved:** 2026-09-13. Report published 22 October 2024. Survey fielded by email 21 May 2024, closed 11 June 2024. 1,033 companies answered. Author: Ulrica Dyrke.

### Quantitative findings (paraphrased)

- 60% of responding members participate in public procurement, up from 47% in the 2020 survey. Almega's own reading is that recession pressure may explain more of the rise than long-term commercial logic.
- Top three reasons for not participating, among the 390 companies that abstain: too much focus on lowest price (34%), time-consuming formalities (24%), too many and irrelevant requirements (22%). Irrelevant requirements rose from 16% to 22%.
- 73% of participating companies are reluctant or fairly reluctant to bid where price dominates, up from 58%.
- 47% now say they are outright reluctant, up from 39%.
- 51% find procurement very or fairly complicated, down from 56%.
- 8% abstain because they do not trust the process to be run properly, up from 7%.
- Participation rises with company size: 85% of firms with 250+ employees participate; among firms with 1–9 employees it is an even split.
- 62% say dialogue before procurement is never or seldom good. 9% say always or often, up from 5%.
- Average bids per advertised procurement in Sweden: 4.8 overall, 5.9 in service procurements — down from 7.1.
- In service procurements, 19% attracted exactly one bid and 20% attracted two: 39% drew no more than two.

### Observations

- `[VERIFIED]` The 300-to-1000 hours line is the best available candidate for a low position with texture rather than category. It is a specific person describing a specific four-year change with an explicit statement that nothing improved. https://www.almega.se/app/uploads/2024/10/Rapport-Offentlig-upphandling-ur-tjansteforetagens-perspektiv.pdf
- `[VERIFIED]` The recurring complaint is not "we can't write" — it is "we already proved this and are being asked to prove it again." That is a documents-system problem, not a copywriting problem. Section "Förenklingsreformen hittills utan tydlig effekt för företagen".
- `[VERIFIED]` The requirement to have already delivered to the public sector is named by suppliers as an irrelevant barrier, and the same barrier is named in the EU reform draft as something to be curbed. Two independent sources, one from each side. See `trigger-eu-public-procurement-act.md`.
- `[INFERRED]` "We manage without them" is a real, articulated version of *do nothing* in this market, and it is stated with pride rather than regret. That changes what an opening is allowed to imply about the reader's competence. Rests on the free-text answers in "Krångelfaktorn består".
- `[INFERRED]` Reluctance to bid on price-dominated tenders rose 15 points in roughly four years while the EU is simultaneously legislating quality floors. If both hold, there is a window where the manufacturers who withdrew are the ones the new rules were designed to bring back. **Unverified as a causal link; the two facts come from different sources and different years.**
- `[VERIFIED]` **Contradiction to record.** Almega gives 4.8 average bids per advertised Swedish procurement (2022 data). TED-based EU figures give 3.2 average bidders per tender (2021). These are different populations and different counting rules, not necessarily a conflict, but they cannot be used in the same sentence. See `notes-market-structure-and-competition.md`.

**Image note.** The report's 15 diagrams are images and I cannot read them. Diagram 3 (reasons for not participating) and Diagram 11 (reluctance by price focus) carry the survey's load. Worth a screenshot from the PDF.

## Voice — FICIL, Foreign Investors Council in Latvia

**What it is:** An investors' council speaking on behalf of foreign companies operating in Latvia, about the Latvian public procurement system. Buyer-adjacent rather than buyer-side: it is companies talking, but through an association's register.
**URL:** https://eng.lsm.lv/article/economy/business/06.06.2025-latvias-public-procurement-still-has-problems-with-favouritism-corruption-and-unfairness-say-investors.a602141/
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-13. Article dated 6 June 2025. Publisher: LSM, Latvian Public Service Media.

---

### Source material (extracted)

Substance, paraphrased:

- FICIL states Latvia needs deep reforms to remove favouritism, corruption risk and unfair practice from public procurement, while acknowledging significant progress in recent years.
- FICIL states its concerns about fairness and transparency in Latvia are much more pronounced than in Estonia and Lithuania.
- 2023 procurement contract value: EUR 5.4 billion excluding VAT, approximately 14% of GDP.
- In 2021–2023, the winner of 73% of procurements was selected solely on lowest price.
- Named problems: complex and fragmented rules raising administrative burden and cost; insufficient professional capacity; lack of transparency and inconsistent practice; a tendency to choose low price over quality.
- FICIL states favouritism risk is higher in energy, telecommunications, transport and other natural-resource-related sectors, citing a telecoms procurement in Sigulda municipality.
- Separate concern raised about long-term relationships between state institutions and specific companies undermining independent assessment, with a named case involving the Ministry of Economics and an institute at the University of Latvia. FICIL states it received no response.
- Quoted spokesperson: Māris Butāns, FICIL's fair competition and public procurement expert and PwC Legal Senior Manager.

One quote, FICIL on the downstream effect: "These problems discourage companies from participating in public procurement, weakening competition and reducing the quality of projects."

---

### Observations

- `[VERIFIED]` 73% lowest-price-only awards in Latvia, 2021–2023, is the hardest number in this pack for the Baltic half of the geography. Same URL.
- `[VERIFIED]` FICIL explicitly ranks Latvia worse than Estonia and Lithuania on fairness perception. The three Baltic states are not one market for this purpose and should not be addressed as one. Same URL.
- `[INFERRED]` If 73% of Latvian awards turn on price alone, a documents-and-reputation offer has a much smaller addressable slice in Latvia than the EU quality-floor narrative implies. The reform that would change this applies 2029–2030 at the earliest. Rests on combining the FICIL figure with the timeline in `trigger-eu-public-procurement-act.md`.
- `[INFERRED]` The trust objection in this market may not be "will this work" but "does any of it matter when the outcome is decided elsewhere." That is a belief-in-self objection with a political edge, and no guarantee touches it. **Unverified — no Latvian or Lithuanian manufacturer was heard from directly. See closing questions.**
- `[VERIFIED]` Follow-up worth fetching, **not retrieved:** LSM, "Ministries given one month to prepare procurement reviews", 26 August 2026 — https://eng.lsm.lv/article/economy/economy/26.08.2026-ministries-given-one-month-to-prepare-procurement-reviews.a660407/ — a live Latvian reform event three weeks before this research, likely trigger material.
- `[VERIFIED]` Primary source behind the participation figure, **not retrieved:** State Audit Office of Latvia, https://www.lrvk.gov.lv/en/news/a-structural-reform-will-be-implemented-to-make-the-public-procurement-system-more-effective

### What this means

`[VERIFIED]` Of the sixteen sources in this pack, fourteen were published by someone selling something or by a regulator. Two carry supplier language: Almega (Swedish service companies, not manufacturers) and FICIL (Latvian foreign investors, through an association).

`[VERIFIED]` **Zero sources in this pack are an industrial manufacturer in north or northeast Europe describing, in their own words, a tender they lost or a partnership they failed to open.** That is the exact population the offer is for.

`[INFERRED]` The absence is probably structural rather than a search failure. Losing a public tender is commercially embarrassing, the buyer is a government you will bid to again next year, and in small Nordic and Baltic markets everyone knows everyone. People do not post about it. Rests on the consistency of the gap across five differently-phrased searches in three languages.

---

### The unexplored seam worth one more day

`[INFERRED]` Procurement complaint and appeal records are the one public archive where a supplier is legally required to state, in their own words and on the record, exactly why they believe they lost. They were not reached in this pass and they are the closest available substitute for interviews:

- **Norway — KOFA**, Klagenemnda for offentlige anskaffelser. Published complaints and decisions.
- **Finland — Markkinaoikeus**, the Market Court. Published procurement judgments.
- **Sweden — förvaltningsrätt / kammarrätt** overprövning judgments, and Konkurrensverket's supervision decisions.
- **Denmark — Klagenævnet for Udbud.**
- **Latvia — Iepirkumu uzraudzības birojs** complaint decisions.

These are in national languages, and the language in them will be legal rather than conversational. But the complainant's stated grievance is usually reproduced, and it is the reader's own account of the loss.

`[VERIFIED]` One related seam partially opened and worth more: Upphandling24 (https://upphandling24.se/tag/norge/) carries Nordic trade-press coverage including a Norwegian report in which startups tell procurers directly to stop specifying the solution. Worth fetching.

### Observations

- `[VERIFIED]` The dominant register across every section is **not "we cannot
  write" but "we already proved this and are being asked to prove it again."**
  Three independent sections carry it. That is a documents-system complaint, and
  it is the closest thing in this file to the offer's own premise.
- `[VERIFIED]` **The withdrawal is stated with pride, repeatedly, and in the
  first person plural.** "Vi klarar oss utan dem", "vi har slutat med LOU-affärer
  helt". This is *do nothing* as an identity, not as a failure. Any opening that
  implies the reader has been beaten will lose the people who talk like this.
- `[VERIFIED]` **Contradiction to record, and it is inside one survey.** The same
  population says quality requirements are so weak that anyone can clear them
  ("kan alla som lämnar anbud hoppa jämfota över") *and* that requirements are so
  heavy only large or incumbent firms can meet them ("bara stora bolag eller
  bolag som redan har avtal kan uppfylla"). Both are printed as representative
  free-text. Not resolved here. The likely reading is that they describe
  different procurements, but the report does not say so.
- `[VERIFIED]` Pre-tender dialogue is described by suppliers as **unpaid
  consulting** in the same breath as it is described as the only chance to
  understand the buyer. That ambivalence is a live objection, not a settled one.

**Section 6, the high position, is still empty.** This is the honest headline of

consistency of the gap across five differently-shaped searches in four languages.

# What the five conversations still have to carry

Desk research has now been run twice, the second time in the right languages and
against the right archives, and the shape of what it cannot reach is clear.

**1. The entire high position. Section 6.** Nothing in two passes and four
languages describes the state after. If copy is drafted now, someone will invent
it, and rung 05 names exactly what that produces — a vivid low position, an
abstract high one, and a reader who feels accused rather than pulled. **This is
the first thing to ask about and it needs a specific past event, not a
projection:** *tell me about a tender where the documents were not the problem —
what was different about that one?*

**2. Whether the reader knows why they lose.** The profile's most expensive
inference is that they have been losing for years and never found out why. Every
complainant located in this pass believes they know precisely why they lost and
argues it in detail. Either the profile is wrong, or the complainants are a
self-selecting minority who complain *because* they know — and the silent
majority is the reader. Desk research cannot tell these apart. A conversation can,
in one question.

**3. What a manufacturer's week actually looks like.** Section 1 still has
`[MISSING]` against it, and after two passes nothing in any language describes a
week at a 50–250 person Nordic manufacturer. Who assembles the submission, from
what, at what hour, and what it is assembled out of.

**4. Whether "document standard" reads as the cure or as more of the disease.**
The Företagarna respondent who called complicated quality management systems an
unreasonable requirement is the warning. A reader who has just called
documentation a burden may hear this offer as another burden with a price on it.
Nothing in the sources resolves which way that lands, and it decides the opening.

**5. The decision mechanics of section 9, which remain almost entirely empty.**
Who signs, who can veto without signing, and what has to be shown to whom. Two
passes have produced exactly one handle — the annual Achilles renewal — and one
proposal that is not law.

Five conversations still beat a third pass. The difference is that the first two
sections of the profile can now be written from evidence, and the conversations
can be spent on the parts that were never going to be published by anyone.
