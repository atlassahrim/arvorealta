## What changed from DR-001's email, and why

Three things. The first was the owner's, and the other two follow from it.

**Three paragraphs instead of five, and an exclusivity frame.** The owner's
proposal: *I am actively looking for a company to back in this tender, and I
can support only one.* It is taken because **it is a constraint rather than a
device** — supporting two bidders in the same competition is a real conflict,
and saying so is both true and more credible than scarcity would be.

**It is stated as care and not as refusal, and that was a correction.** The
draft read *Only one. I will not work both sides of a live procurement.* The
owner's objection is that it sounds like a threat, and he is right: it is a
refusal, it implies somebody might ask, and it puts the writer on guard rather
than alongside. `I will support only one company. Two in the same competition
would be no help to either.` is the same constraint said as a reason to trust
him. **`backing` went with it** — backing is what you do to a horse, supporting
is what you do with a company.

**The second paragraph is about the evaluator, not about the reader's
judgement.** It first read *An evaluator can also open what you already
publish and check it against what you wrote.* The owner tried *let's say you
only send what you have published*, which has the right instinct and the
wrong subject: it imagines the reader doing something naive, which is the
thing the paragraph exists to avoid. Naming what the evaluator will have in
front of them puts writer and reader on the same side of the desk and assumes
the reader is competent, which they are — Namdal employ two people whose job
title is tenders.

**`If not, no reply needed.` is gone.** It was there to disarm, and it did,
but it also handed the reader written permission to do nothing. The close now
names a date and asks for a reply before it. **The deadline was already in the
old email and was not the ask; now it is the ask.**

**The wording survives being sent to twelve more names on the same tender.**
*I am backing one bidder* is true however many it goes to, and first-come is
how a single slot works. *I have chosen you* would not be true and is not
written. Keep the line as it stands.

**The two catalogues are named, and the attachment's filename is not.** The
email said *your catalogues*, which is vague enough to read as a mail merge;
naming Schäfer and Craemer proves the documents were opened and cannot be
faked by a template. It also helps the register — **the problems are in the
manufacturers' documents and not in Namdal's work**, and saying the names out
loud makes that clear before a reader can feel accused.

Two things stay out. **The page counts** — 102 and four — are findings, and
findings belong on sheet 1. **The filename** is plumbing: the email already
says *the attached two pages*, which is what actually defuses the worry about
opening a stranger's file, and a filename repeated in the body reads as
machine-written. **`Fritz Schäfer GmbH` stays on the sheet too**, where it is
a source citation; in an email to a man who buys from them it would be stiff.

**What is unchanged, and deliberately.** The count is the one figure that
crosses, because 5 of 34 is a question rather than an answer and cannot be
acted on without opening the file. Figures are numerals here and words in the
document. The email contextualises the attachment and never summarises it —
none of the four findings appears in the body. `Have a nice day.` is the
owner's sign-off and stays as written.

## The premise this was written against, stated once

**The first email was three days old when it was called a non-answer.** It
went to EnviroPac on 6 October. One send and three days is not evidence about
an approach, and the rewrite above is an improvement rather than a repair.
If the new wording outperforms the old one it will take more than one send to
know that too.
