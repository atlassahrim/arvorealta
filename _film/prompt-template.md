Act as a film director and a marketing strategist. Make a short explainer film, about 35 seconds, for a brand, using the story and style in the square brackets below. Build everything in code: no stock footage and no generated images of real clients or real marks.

STORY: [Describe the story in a few sentences: who the main character is, what their ordinary day looks like, what problem the brand solves for them, and the moment it changes. Name the brand and its website.]

STYLE: [Describe the look, or attach reference images: the illustration style, the colour palette, the mood. Example: isometric 2.5D woodblock print, colours sampled from my reference image, washi paper texture, mist bands, a red seal.]

How to work: stop after each step, show me the result, and wait for my approval before going on.

1. Treatment first. Write a one-paragraph logline and story arc about ordinary life. The arc moves from a problem nobody can see, to a turning point, to the same life made visible. Show why the decision-maker needs this: for buyers and documents, for their own sense of identity, and for their people.

2. Script. Exactly 12 phrases, numbered, ready to copy and paste. Each phrase stands alone and closes its own thought; no sentence runs across two phrases. Use the plain, casual tone of a Sunday-morning conversation, in the first person of the main character. Keep each phrase short enough to say in about 2 seconds. The last phrase is the brand line. Every factual claim must be true; invent no statistics.

3. Voice. Generate one continuous take of all 12 phrases, so it flows as one read. Use my voice in ElevenLabs if I give you a voice ID, or a free offline voice if I don't. Use a casual, persuasive register. If it runs long, speed it up to about 1.1 times without changing the pitch. Run speech-to-text on the result, list any word that comes back different, and send me the audio.

4. Three styleframes. Draw three key moments in the STYLE: the opening, the turning point and the ending. Then a cast sheet showing each character at full height.
   - **Line:** straight strokes only, no rounded corners and no circles.
   - **Colour:** at most three colours per character, one per zone (head, body, legs).
   - **Shapes:** legs and arms are triangles, and the shoulders are never wider than the waist.
   - **Proportions:** true to the world around them; never crop a character or blow them up to fill the frame.
   - **Scale:** in wide or isometric views, characters become small three-box block figures (legs, body, head) with a faint ground shadow.
   - **Checks:** before showing anything, look for colours turning dark where printed layers overlap, seams opening between faces, and lines from hidden objects showing through.

5. Test scene. Animate the first two phrases with the voice, so I can judge picture and voice together. Cut on each phrase.

6. Full film. Make one shot per phrase, plus an opening establishing shot: a camera glide across the whole world, landing on the main character just before they speak.
   - Every shot uses the same projection and the same world, with a slow camera drift.
   - The story is told by things that change: a mark appearing, a jacket closing over a name, a document rejected and then kept.
   - Mirror two shots, before and after, so the payoff reads without words.
   - End by pulling back to the whole world, then a closing card with the brand mark, the line and the website.

7. Sound, all free.
   - **Music:** an original score on CC0 instrument samples: a low bed under the problem, a warmer chord at the turning point, a resolution at the end, and a soft note on every cut.
   - **Effects:** synthesised in code, for example water, rain, a zip, paper, a stamp.
   - **Mix:** the voice sits clearly on top.
   - **Checks:** loudness around −16 LUFS, a speech-to-text check of the finished mix, and confirmation that the first word lands just after the first cut.

Rules throughout:
- Render frame by frame at 25 fps, 1920 × 1080, and encode with ffmpeg to H.264 with AAC audio.
- Say plainly what you cannot check yourself, such as hearing the mix.
- Keep a credits file listing every licence used.
- Commit the source files.
- After each step, report in a few lines what was done and what is weakest. End every reply with a two-sentence plain summary.
